Wisdom tooth removal cost without insurance: simple vs impacted teeth

The cost of removing a wisdom tooth depends on how the tooth has erupted and the complexity of removal. Compare published U.S. procedure references, common extra charges and questions for your oral surgeon.

Published November 10, 2026 · By DentaOferta editorial team · General U.S. patient information

Quick answer

CareCredit’s published U.S. price table lists $177 on average for a simple tooth extraction, $363 for a surgical extraction and $532 for a fully bone-impacted tooth. These are single-tooth procedure categories and typically should not be used as a quote for removing all four wisdom teeth. Sedation and diagnostics may add fees.

Published cost by type of wisdom tooth extraction

Wisdom teeth can be fully visible, partially covered by gum tissue or impacted in the jaw. The CareCredit wisdom tooth removal report gives the following published national reference figures for different single-tooth removal categories, with local anesthesia as the basis described for its table.

These labels refer to different procedure complexities. A dentist or oral surgeon will determine the actual classification after evaluation, sometimes with imaging. Published averages are not guaranteed fees.

One wisdom tooth versus four wisdom teeth

A price shown for one tooth cannot simply be multiplied by four to obtain an accurate bundled surgical quote. Different teeth may require different procedures, and a surgeon may charge for anesthesia, clinical services or facility time in ways that are shared across an appointment.

Ask for a line-by-line quote for each tooth, plus any common surgery and sedation charges. The American Dental Association’s wisdom tooth guide explains why dentists evaluate symptoms, position and possible effects on neighboring teeth before recommending removal.

Extra charges that can change the total

  • Consultation and imaging: an examination and appropriate X-rays may be charged separately.
  • Sedation or anesthesia: ask which option is proposed and what its separate fee covers; not every case requires deep sedation.
  • Surgical difficulty: impacted teeth, root anatomy or proximity to important structures can change planning.
  • Medicines and aftercare: ask what medications, post-operative instructions and follow-up visits are included.
  • Facility and specialist fees: confirm whether any additional provider or facility bills are expected.

Worked illustration of an itemized estimate

Illustration only, not a national average: Suppose a written plan lists $600 for a surgical extraction, $180 for examination and imaging, and $400 for an agreed sedation service. The math is $1,180 for that plan. It would be misleading to call the original $600 fee the entire treatment cost. Your exact plan may involve different services and prices.

Do wisdom teeth always need to be removed?

No. Some wisdom teeth remain in place with appropriate monitoring, while others need treatment because of pain, infection, decay, damage to a neighboring tooth, or other clinical findings. The ADA recommends examination and discussion of risks and alternatives. Do not use a cost guide to decide whether an asymptomatic tooth should be removed.

After an extraction, follow the individual instructions from your treating clinician. The ADA extraction guidance explains basic healing precautions, but your surgeon's directions take priority.

Seek urgent care for severe symptoms: persistent major bleeding, rapidly increasing facial swelling, fever or difficulty swallowing or breathing should be assessed promptly; breathing difficulty is an emergency.

How to compare self-pay oral surgery quotes

Without dental insurance, contact dental or oral surgery offices and request the out-of-pocket total for the planned services. Ask whether a quote includes oral surgeon fees and anesthesia, how long it is valid, and whether a payment plan changes the total. With insurance, check dental and any applicable medical benefits with the plan; coverage is not guaranteed simply because surgery is recommended.

Questions before your removal appointment

  • What is the classification and reason for removal of each tooth?
  • Can you provide separate fees for each tooth and common surgical charges?
  • Which imaging is necessary and is it included?
  • What anesthesia or sedation is planned and what will it cost?
  • Are medication, follow-up and management of complications included?
  • Are there reasons to monitor any tooth rather than remove it now?

Frequently asked questions

How much does removing one wisdom tooth cost without insurance?

Published U.S. single-tooth procedure averages range by extraction type, including $177 for a simple extraction and $532 for a full bony impaction in CareCredit's study. Actual charges and extras vary.

Is removing four wisdom teeth four times the single-tooth price?

Not reliably. Tooth complexity differs and a single appointment can have shared or additional anesthesia and facility charges. Ask for the four-tooth treatment plan.

Is sedation always included in the extraction fee?

No. Confirm the type of anesthesia proposed and the separate or included fee before scheduling.

Do all impacted wisdom teeth need immediate surgery?

Not necessarily. Treatment decisions depend on clinical findings, symptoms and risks assessed by a licensed dentist or oral surgeon.
