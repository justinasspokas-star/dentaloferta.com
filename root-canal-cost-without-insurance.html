Root canal cost without insurance: price by tooth and fees to check

A root canal fee may not include the crown, core build-up or diagnostics. Review published U.S. costs and learn which questions help you compare complete treatment plans.

Published November 10, 2026 · By DentaOferta editorial team · General U.S. patient information

Quick answer

A September 2026 CareCredit report gives a published U.S. average of $978 for root canal treatment, with a reported $679–$1,733 range for the overall category. The same report gives averages of $905 for a front tooth, $1,023 for a premolar and $1,220 for a molar. These are reference figures, not personalized clinic quotes and may exclude restoration.

What may be added to the root canal bill?

Root canal treatment addresses infected or damaged tissue inside a tooth. Depending on the tooth and its condition, a dentist may recommend a final filling, a core build-up or a dental crown afterward. A complete written estimate should identify each separate charge.

  • Examination: emergency visit or consultation before treatment.
  • Imaging: necessary X-rays or other imaging based on clinical need.
  • Root canal procedure: identify the tooth and whether one or multiple visits are expected.
  • Final restoration: permanent filling, build-up, post or crown as applicable.
  • Specialist and follow-up: ask whether an endodontist, retreatment or further visits may add cost.

Example: procedure fee versus a full plan

Illustration only: A dentist may propose $1,000 for root canal therapy, $1,250 for a final crown, $180 for an exam and imaging, and $200 for an additional build-up. The illustrative total is $2,630, not $1,000. Another patient may not need the same restoration. The example is not a market average or a DentaOferta quote.

What happens during treatment?

In general, a dentist or endodontist removes inflamed or infected pulp tissue, cleans and seals the canal system, and then restores the tooth. The goal is to preserve a tooth when clinically feasible. The Cleveland Clinic’s medically reviewed overview explains the steps and why some teeth need a protective permanent restoration. The dentist must decide which approach is appropriate after examination.

When to seek urgent care: Significant facial swelling, fever associated with dental infection, difficulty swallowing or difficulty breathing require prompt evaluation. Trouble breathing or swallowing may be an emergency; seek urgent medical attention rather than comparing prices online.

Root canal versus extraction: not just the first bill

A simple extraction may have a lower upfront fee, but a missing tooth can create separate replacement decisions and costs. A root canal may involve a crown and maintenance. The clinical choice should not be made from a web price list.

Our root canal versus extraction guide explains the different goals and patient questions; the extraction cost guide covers self-pay extraction line items.

Paying without dental insurance

Request an itemized self-pay quote and ask whether an office offers phased payment or financing. Clarify the cost of financing and any penalties before signing. If you have dental insurance, ask about endodontic coverage, deductibles, annual maximums and the coverage of any subsequent crown. A pretreatment estimate may help, but it is not a guarantee of insurer payment.

Written quote checklist

  • Which tooth is being treated and what is the proposed diagnosis?
  • What is the root canal procedure fee and does it cover all visits?
  • Are X-rays, consultation or emergency charges separate?
  • What permanent restoration is recommended and how much does it cost?
  • Will a general dentist or endodontist perform the work?
  • Could the estimate change after treatment begins, and why?

Frequently asked questions

How much does a root canal cost without insurance?

A September 2026 national report gives a $978 overall average and $679–$1,733 reported range for the root canal category. The real total depends on your tooth, treatment and any additional restoration.

Are molar root canals generally more expensive?

The cited report gives higher average procedure costs for molars than front teeth, but individual quotes vary. More complex canal anatomy and treatment needs can affect fees.

Is the crown included in the root canal cost?

Not necessarily. Always ask for the cost of the recommended permanent restoration as a separate line item if it is not included.

Should I extract the tooth instead to save money?

Cost alone cannot establish the safer or more suitable treatment. A dentist should explain which options are clinically feasible and what each complete treatment plan requires.
