Dental bone graft cost for implants: prices, procedures and extra fees

A bone graft may be part of some implant plans, but graft type and complexity can change the bill. Understand published U.S. cost categories and what your estimate should include.

Published November 10, 2026 · By DentaOferta editorial team · General U.S. patient information

Quick answer

CareCredit’s published U.S. cost tables list distinct national procedure averages, including $558 for a socket graft, $811 for an allograft and $2,022 for a sinus lift. These are different procedures—not interchangeable choices or complete implant prices. Whether you need grafting is a clinical decision.

What is a dental bone graft and when might it be discussed?

A dental bone graft is material placed to support the growth or preservation of bone in an area that has lost volume. A dentist or specialist may consider grafting after a tooth extraction, before certain implants, or during treatment for other types of bone loss. Not all implant patients need grafts.

The Cleveland Clinic patient guide explains common graft material types and their clinical purpose. The specific choice depends on examination, imaging when appropriate, anatomy and your health history—not on cost alone.

How bone grafting changes the total implant quote

If a graft is needed, it can affect treatment staging and the price quoted for implant care. A written plan should distinguish implant placement, graft surgery, graft material, membrane if used, anesthesia, diagnostics, abutment and final crown. Some clinics package portions together; others itemize them.

Our implant cost without insurance article explains the main restoration components, and our implant quote calculator lets you enter clinic-specific fees instead of assuming every patient requires a graft.

Worked example: comparing two hypothetical plans

Mathematical illustration only: Imagine a base implant proposal totaling $3,200. If a dentist determines that grafting is required and the written graft charge is $800, the combined total is $4,000 before any other additions. A different patient may not need grafting at all. These illustrative figures are not a price estimate, a procedure average or medical advice.

Ask whether a quoted “graft fee” includes membrane materials, graft placement, reviews and any separate surgical-site services.

What to ask about recovery and treatment timing

Graft healing and the timing of later implant treatment differ among patients and procedures. The Cleveland Clinic notes that early recovery and full bone integration are not the same thing, and healing may continue for many months. Only the treating clinician can advise on when an implant may be suitable.

Questions worth asking include: Will the graft and implant be performed at one appointment or in stages? How will healing be assessed? What is the plan if additional grafting becomes necessary? Who should you contact if pain, swelling, fever or other symptoms worsen?

Safety: New or worsening facial swelling, fever, severe pain, or trouble swallowing or breathing need prompt assessment. Do not delay professional care to obtain an online cost comparison.

Paying for grafting without insurance

Ask for a current, written self-pay estimate broken down by clinically recommended procedures. If insured, request plan-specific confirmation of coverage, deductibles, preauthorization rules, waiting periods and annual maximums. Financial assistance or financing offers should be assessed by their full repayment cost, not only a monthly payment.

Bone grafting estimate checklist

  • Why is grafting recommended for my particular situation?
  • Which grafting procedure is proposed and what alternatives, if any, are appropriate?
  • Are graft materials, membrane, anesthesia and follow-ups included?
  • Are extraction, CBCT or other imaging fees separate?
  • When might implant placement be considered and could there be additional visits?
  • What is the written total for the entire implant plan, not only the graft?

Frequently asked questions

How much does a dental bone graft cost before an implant?

The published U.S. references vary by procedure: for example $558 for a socket graft and $811 for an allograft in CareCredit’s cost table. A clinician must first decide whether and which graft is indicated.

Does everyone getting a dental implant need a bone graft?

No. The need for grafting depends on bone support and other individual clinical factors assessed by a dentist or specialist.

Is a sinus lift the same as a socket graft?

No. They are different surgical procedures that should not be treated as interchangeable price options.

Does the graft quote include the implant and crown?

Not necessarily. Confirm that the written treatment plan lists implant surgery, abutment and crown fees separately or explicitly includes them.
