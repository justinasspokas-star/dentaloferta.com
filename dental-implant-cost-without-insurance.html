Dental implant cost without insurance: how to compare the full price

If you are paying out of pocket for a dental implant, the number in an ad may not be the total on your treatment plan. Use this guide to understand a published U.S. price reference and compare written estimates, item by item.

Quick answer: A U.S. pricing report published by CareCredit in February 2026 lists an average of $2,143 for a full-size single-tooth implant, with a reported range of $1,646–$4,157. Its underlying procedure-cost research was conducted in 2024. These figures are not quotes from DentaOferta clinics and should not be assumed to cover every component of your replacement tooth. Read the source and its methodology.

What these numbers mean: They are sourced reference values, not estimates for your exact case. Ask a dentist whether a quote includes the implant post, abutment, crown, examination, imaging, extraction, bone grafting, anesthesia, and follow-up.

What makes two implant quotes different?

A single-tooth replacement may involve multiple services: evaluating bone and gum health, planning the procedure, surgically placing an implant, attaching a connector (abutment), and restoring the tooth with a crown. Depending on your examination, additional procedures may be discussed. Not every clinic packages or itemizes these the same way.

  • Exam and diagnostics: Is the consultation included? Will any radiographs or 3D imaging be recommended, and what do they cost?
  • Implant placement: Is the surgical placement fee separate from the cost of the implant device?
  • Abutment and crown: Does the advertised price include both, or will they be invoiced later?
  • Additional procedures: Are extraction, grafting, periodontal treatment, and temporary restoration extra?
  • Aftercare: Are follow-up appointments and adjustments included? Who handles complications?

The American Dental Association's 2026 guidance emphasizes that dental imaging should be selected according to the individual's clinical needs after examination—not ordered automatically or based on photos alone. ADA imaging recommendations.

Example: why a lower headline price can be misleading

Imagine that Office A advertises an implant placement fee but lists the crown, abutment, and examination separately. Office B quotes a higher initial amount that includes those items. You cannot determine which is less expensive simply by comparing the first numbers. Always compare the total proposed treatment plan for equivalent clinical services. This is a hypothetical comparison; it is not a claim about any clinic's current prices.

Costs in Miami, Hialeah, Doral, and elsewhere in Florida

Location influences the prices clinics charge. The cited CareCredit research lists a Florida state-level average of $2,130 for a full-size single-tooth implant; it is not a Miami or Hialeah quote and it is not evidence that a particular local clinic charges that amount. To compare local prices, request an itemized written estimate from providers you are considering. For an independent local benchmark, FAIR Health Consumer offers a U.S. ZIP-code dental cost lookup.

Does insurance cover dental implants?

Coverage depends on your particular plan, including exclusions, network requirements, deductibles, annual maximums, and waiting periods. Even if a procedure is covered in part, your out-of-pocket amount can differ from the quoted office fee. The ADA explains common plan limitations. Ask the office and insurer for a pretreatment estimate if available, rather than assuming coverage.

How to ask for a price estimate

  1. Identify clinics you wish to contact and confirm they are licensed for the service you need.
  2. Ask what information they need before offering an estimate. Photos may be useful for organizing a request but cannot determine the diagnosis.
  3. Ask for the same itemized cost categories from each clinic.
  4. Clarify which fees may change after an in-person examination, imaging, or a different treatment recommendation.
  5. Choose based on clinical suitability, experience, communication, and transparent costs—not only the lowest price.

Frequently asked questions

Is $2,143 the full price of a dental implant and crown?

Not necessarily. It is a published reference value for a full-size single-tooth implant, not a quote for your complete treatment. Ask the clinic whether the abutment, crown, necessary imaging, and any extra procedures are included.

Can a dentist give an exact price from a photo?

Photos may help communicate a concern, but cannot replace a professional examination or imaging when clinically necessary. Final diagnosis and treatment planning require evaluation by a licensed dentist.

Are implants always the lowest-cost option?

No. A clinician may discuss other suitable options depending on your oral health and missing-tooth situation. Comparing alternatives should include the entire care plan and ongoing maintenance.

What if I do not have dental insurance?

Ask for a cash-pay or self-pay estimate in writing. If a clinic offers financing, review terms and total repayment cost before agreeing.
